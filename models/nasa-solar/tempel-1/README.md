# Comet 9P/Tempel 1

`original.obj` is the [NASA JPL NAIF archived OBJ](https://naif.jpl.nasa.gov/pub/naif/ROSETTA/kernels/dsk/TEMPEL1_9P_K032_THO_V01.OBJ); SHA-256 `a5380663eeb291cfc4e3bb3a7368de73c74923beaa858352c3bb326ff776f36a`. Its 16,022 vertices and 32,040 faces match the [NASA PDS plate model](https://pds.nasa.gov/ds-view/pds/viewProfile.jsp?dsid=DIF-C-HRIV/ITS/MRI-5-TEMPEL1-SHAPE-V2.0) derived from Deep Impact and Stardust-NExT images. Cite T. L. Farnham and P. C. Thomas, PLATE SHAPE MODEL OF COMET 9P/TEMPEL 1 V2.0, NASA PDS, 2013. The [NAIF archive readme](https://naif.jpl.nasa.gov/pub/naif/ROSETTA/kernels/dsk/aareadme.txt) identifies the model and origin. [NASA's scientific-data policy](https://science.data.nasa.gov/about/license) describes the CC0 default for unmarked NASA-led mission data; the NASA Open Data Portal lists this dataset without a specific license.

`convert.py` imports the OBJ into Blender with auto-execution disabled, smooths normals, adds a neutral illustrative material, centers and scales it, and exports `model.glb`. It contains the nucleus only, without a tail or surface image. The PDS description states that around 70% is constrained by control points; other regions are less certain. The color and lighting are presentation choices. Run with the installed Blender: `blender --background --factory-startup --disable-autoexec --python models/nasa-solar/tempel-1/convert.py`.
