# Studying a chibi head in Blender

An oblique portrait shows whether the expression reads. A side view separates the depth of the facial features from the head shape and reveals how far the fringe projects. Keep the camera, framing and lighting fixed while toggling the hair and facial features. The difference between the two renders tells the artist which mesh to edit.

## Two published examples

The local Codex skill `blender-character-source-study` is registered in KB-MCP as `blender:character-source-study`. Its read-only tool `blender_inspect_saved_collections` opens a supplied Blender file in a separate process and reports collection/object matches. Use that inventory to select subjects for the profile renders. The personal Blender KB holds separate records for the Ranyi source study and the Indigo guide-edit and visual repair experiment.

**Ranyi Chibi Maid.** Blender 5.1.2 opened the published `chibi_maid_USER.blend` with automatic script execution disabled. It reported that 5.2.45 wrote the file. In the isolated oblique view, the fringe overlaps the upper eye area and separate eye highlights carry the gaze. The side view shows the temple lock in front of the rear hair mass. Hiding the features and hair at the same camera angle reveals a rounded skull and a shallow forward swell in the lower face. Workbench material colors simplify the iris shading. The source hash matched before and after inspection. [Original Blender file](https://github.com/RYranyi/dsh_blender_ranyimaid/blob/59816e725a3258d3d63c168c12909ed5311792d0/chibi_maid_USER.blend) · [Local captures, attribution and settings](../../output/ninja-head-study/ranyi/README.md)

To connect a visible feature to its construction, isolate `01_Head` and `02_Hair` in a fresh Blender window and put the right-facing orthographic profile beside a Text Editor. Load `src/mhair.py` as text and go to line 216. In this file, `build_fringe_mass` varies the lower edge of a single grid across four waves, raises it at the temples and gives the surface thickness. This explains the scalloped forehead edge visible beside the separate pointed temple lock. ## Apply the study to an original character

1. Inspect a reference file in a separate Blender process with `--factory-startup --disable-autoexec`. Record the source revision and license. Select the head, face and hair collections. Render an oblique view, a profile and the bare head at the same profile angle.
2. Choose the defining features of the new design. For the Indigo Ninja they are the swept blue spikes, exposed brow and sideways amber gaze. Fix the ear, brow, hairline and nape positions on a shared head blockout before building the face and hair separately.
3. Check the assembled head from the front, in three-quarter view and in profile. At portrait size, check the pupil direction and the smile. At thumbnail size, trace the outer hair silhouette. Give each correction an angle, a named feature and an observable result, such as exposing the brow by lifting the fringe above it.
4. Export the character and render the actual GLB at the same angles. Keep the source render and exported render together when diagnosing a missing feature.

In the first Indigo Ninja portrait the eyes read as a forward gaze. The front GLB view also showed a jagged patch where two scarf meshes crossed. The revision shifted both irises toward the character's left, narrowed the opposite upper lid and built both scarf colors into one folded surface. The same hero camera now shows the sideways glance and an uninterrupted scarf seam. Compare the two portraits, then use the runtime views and build instructions in the asset record.
